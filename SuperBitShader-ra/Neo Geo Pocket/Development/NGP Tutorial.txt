The Neo Geo Pocket Hardware Explained
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The Neo Geo Pocket is a handheld that uses 8 monochrome shades, with 4-6 at a time in practice. That being said there is not a lot of tools for this hardware so take 4-6 with a grain of salt.

2. A real Neo Geo Pocket Color has input that looks different when processed through the Neo Geo Pocket Colors Display, these color palettes are designed to match that output with in a 2.2 hue margin of error or less for both color correction and mixed-frames.

3. Also a real Neo Geo Pocket has interframe blending for transparency, this effect is pre-baked into Super-Bit-Shader.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
How to Make your own Palette
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. First open one of the included Mixed-Frames samples, in this tutorials case choose Sunset.
retroarch/shaders/SuperBitShader-ra/Neo Geo Pocket/Development/Mixed Frames - Neo Geo Pocket/

2. Now with Sunset opened in Gimp you will see each color out of the palette labeled.

- W = White (This is Same Across All Palettes)
- 1 (This is Same Across All Palettes)
- 2
- 3
- 4
- 5
- 6 (This is Same Across All Palettes)
- B = Black (This is Same Across All Palettes)

3. This version of the palettes have the colors blown out, without it the shader would look dull when combined with "mixed_frames".

4. If you want to go extra authentic do research on what your system can output color wise after color correction.

5. To make adjustments more managable, I recommend leaving "W,1,6 and B" alone and only tweak "2,3,4, and 5" hue's as you see fit.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Importing the Palette into Retroarch
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Make a copy of "SBS - Neo Geo Pocket (8-bit) - Sunset.glsl" in the following directory:
retroarch\shaders\SuperBitShader-ra\Neo Geo Pocket\shader-files

2. Rename Sunset into your preferred palette name.
Example: "SBS - Neo Geo Pocket (8-bit) - PaletteNameHERE.glsl

3. Open the file in a text editor, locate the "Palette Settings", then use the provided Hex to Vec3 converter to apply your colors.
https://airtightinteractive.com/util/hex-to-glsl/

3. Save the file and close it. Now it's time to change directories to the following:
retroarch\shaders\SuperBitShader-ra\Neo Geo Pocket

4. Make a copy of "4. SBS - Neo Geo Pocket Sunset.glslp" and rename it to your preferred palette name.
Example: "5. SBS - Neo Geo Pocket PaletteNameHERE.glslp"

5. Open the GLSLP file in a text editor and change Sunset to your palettes name as well.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
How to Make your own Palette - Testing the Palette
----------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Load the "144p Suite" from Retroarch's Beetle Wonderswan Core.

2. Access shaders from the Quick Menu and load your shader preset to test.

3. If it looks good, you are all set! :)
